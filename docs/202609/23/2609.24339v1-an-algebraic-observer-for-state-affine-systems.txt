Abstract 

We present a new systematic method to design an algebraic swapping lemma observer (Bobtsov et al., 2026b) for nonlinear systems which are affine in the state. Four are the main features of the new observer: (i) Far superior transient performance compared to standard asymptotically convergent observers; (ii) structural simplicity—its construction relies solely on basic linear filtering and a single matrix inversion; (iii) applicability to non-uniformly-completely-observable systems, for which classical methods are not applicable; (iv) straightforward extension to the cases of unknown time-varying parameters and measurement delay. The core idea exploited in the paper is to combine the construction of a generalized parameter estimation-based observer (Ortega et al., 2021b) with a simple filtering technique to derive the algebraic observer. The resulting design is shown to apply to a class of state-affine systems satisfying a weak generic condition . Moreover, it is established that for time-invariant systems, this condition is always satisfied globally. 

Key words: Observer design, Adaptive observers, Algebraic observer, State-affine systems 

1 Introduction and Problem Formulation 

A class of nonlinear systems for which the problem of designing a state observer is well-known, are the so-called state-affine systems, whose dynamics is described by (Bernard, 2019, Equation 3.1): ˙x = A(y, u )x + B(y, u )

y = h(x, u ), (1) where x(t) ∈ Rn is the unmeasurable state, y(t) ∈ Rp

denotes the measured output signal and u(t) ∈ Rm is the control input. When the read-out map is linear in x,that is, h(x, u ) = Cx the most famous observer used for this kind of systems is the Kalman-Bucy’s Filter (KBF) introduced in (Kalman and Bucy, 1961) for linear time-varying (LTV) systems—which are obtained evaluating the matrices A, B and C of the system above along the input and output trajectories. That is, rewriting the sys-tem (1) as the LTV system ˙x = A(t)x + B(t)uy = Cx, (2) 

⋆ Corresponding author: Anton Pyrkin (pyrkin@itmo.ru). 

with A(t) ∈ Rn×n, B (t) ∈ Rn×m and C ∈ Rp×n are defined via 

A(t) := A(y(t), u (t)) , B (t) := B(y(t), u (t)) , C := C.

It is well-known that to ensure global convergence for this observer it is necessary to impose very strong ex-citation assumptions on the system, namely, uniform complete observability (UCO) of the pair ( A(t), B (t)) (Bernard, 2019; Kalman and Bucy, 1961). In a recent paper (Wang et al., 2023) it was shown that state ob-servation of LTV systems is possible imposing only the (necessary) assumption of observability . For, it is pro-posed to use the recently reported generalized parame-ter estimation-based observers (GPEBO) (Ortega et al., 2021b). The main feature of GPEBO is that the problem of state observation is recast as a problem of parame-ter estimation, namely of the systems initial conditions. This approach has proven to be very successful for the state observation of state-affine systems, and many ex-tensions and practical applications to the method have been reported (Bezzubov et al., 2022; Bobtsov et al., 2021a,b; Pyrkin et al., 2023; Romero and Ortega, 2023). In the recent publication (Bobtsov et al., 2026b) a 

Preprint submitted to Automatica 22 September 2026 arXiv:2609.24339v1 [eess.SY] 21 Sep 2026 novel approach to the problem of state observation was reported. The main feature of these new observers is that we center our attention in the design of algebraic observers —the qualifier “algebraic” meaning that we want to obtain an algebraic relation between the system state and filtered versions of the systems inputs and outputs, which holds true for all t ≥ 0. The algebraic property should be contrasted with the usual procedure of designing a dynamical system whose output (the state estimate) asymptotically (or in fixed/finite time) converges to the systems state. As shown in (Bobtsov et al., 2026b) a key component in the design of this algebraic observer is the application of the Swapping Lemma (Sastry and Bodson, 1989, Lemma 3.6.5), hence we give to the new observer the name Algebraic Swap-ping Lemma Observer (ASLO). A key feature of the ASLO is that, in contrast with stan-dard asymptotically convergent observers, its transient behavior is not dependent on the prior knowledge of the systems initial state. Indeed, in classical observer the ini-tial value of the state estimation error ˜ x(t) := ˆ x(t) − x(t)is clearly ˆ x(0) −x(0), where ˆ x(t) is the state of a dynamic system-based observer, whose initial conditions are cho-sen by the designer. In ASLO, as the construction of the estimate is algebraic—and not via the construction of an auxiliary dynamical system—such a direct dependence is conspicuous by its absence. The application of ASLO for general nonlinear systems of the form ˙ x = f (x, u ), y = h(x) is restricted to the case when the derivative of some of the states is known —or a new state can be constructed for which this property is verified (Bobtsov et al., 2026b, Assumption 1). Clearly, the construction of such an “auxiliary” state involves the solution of a partial differential equation that, as is well known, is hard to obtain. The main contribution of this paper is the proof that for the particular case of state-affine systems (1) it is always possible to design an ASLO, and an explicit, systematic procedure to do it is given in the paper. The main idea exploited in the paper is to combine the construction of a GPEBO (Ortega et al., 2021b) with a simple filtering technique to derive the ASLO. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Sec-tion 2 gives the main result. In Section 3 we consider the case of LTI systems. In Section 4 we apply the method to solve the challenging problem of state estimation of systems which are not UCO or their states converge to non observable ones. Section 5 presents some simulation results for LTI and LTV systems. In Section 6 we present extensions of the result to the cases of systems with measurement delay, unknown parameters and switching parameters. The paper is wrapped-up with concluding remarks in Section 7. The proof of some propositions, which are technically burdensome, are given in Appen-dices A and B.

7 Concluding Remarks 

The newly introduced concept of an algebraic observer represents a major breakthrough in the field of observer design. A class of algebraic observer has been derived in an extensive series of publications, including (Fliess et al., 2008; Sira-Ram´ ırez et al., 2014). In these works its unquestionable superiority vis-` a-vis classical asymptotic observers is discussed at length. Moreover, it is argued that the availability of algebraic observers trivializes the control design task. Unfortunately, in all these works the authors rely on the operation of successive signal differentiation —an operation that may be problematic for practical applications where the presence of noise is unavoidable. This undesirable feature is conspicuously absent from our ASLO approach. We present four key features of algebraic observers to demonstrate their superiority: 

F1 Far superior transient performance, stemming from the fact that the estimated state signal be-10 comes aligned with the true state almost immedi-ately and, under a generic rank condition on an algorithm-generated matrix, remains exact for all subsequent time. This stands in sharp contrast to classical asymptotic (or finite/fixed-time) conver-gent designs. 

F2 Structural simplicity: the construction relies solely on basic linear filtering and a single matrix inver-sion. These computational requirements compare favorably with the ≈ 4n3 + 4 n2m + 2 nm 2 flops per time step required by the KBF (Sandberg et al., 2014). 

F3 Applicability to non-UCO systems , or systems with trajectories converging to unobservable states, for which classical methods such as the KBF are not applicable—see Section 4 and (Bobtsov et al., 2026a). 

F4 As shown in Section 6 it is straightforward to extend our design to several challenging situations, e.g. ,unknown time-varying or switching parameters as well as output measurement with time-varying de-lay. None of these cases can be handled with exist-ing observers. As discussed in (Bobtsov et al., 2026b), the applica-tion of ASLO to general nonlinear systems of the form ˙x = f (x, u ), y = h(x), is restricted to cases where the 

derivative of certain state components are known . Fur-thermore, the design proceeds on a case-by-case basis, with no unified systematic methodology. In contrast to this situation, for the particular class of state-affine sys-tems , we show in this paper that an ASLO can always be constructed and will be applicable under weak generic conditions. Moreover, we provide a simple, step-by-step, systematic design procedure that requires only the op-eration of linear filtering and a matrix inversion.
