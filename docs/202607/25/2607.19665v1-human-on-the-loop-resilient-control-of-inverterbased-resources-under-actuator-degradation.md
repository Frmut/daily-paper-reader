## 摘要
本文针对执行器退化情况下的电网支撑型基于逆变器的资源（IBRs），提出了一种“人在回路”（human-on-the-loop）弹性控制架构。在此背景下，传统的容错控制（FTC）和自适应控制策略均面临显著局限性：主动式 FTC 依赖于快速、准确的故障检测与隔离，容易受到早期或模糊退化误诊的影响；而被动式 FTC 则往往趋于过度保守。自适应控制器也面临类似问题，因为它们通常假设具备充足的控制权限，但当执行器退化削弱该权限时，自适应律可能会误解跟踪误差，导致参数漂移、性能损失或不稳定。为克服这些局限性，所提出的框架将人类监督判断直接嵌入控制回路，用于检测细微的非标行为、验证或覆盖控制器参数，并在工况超出建模故障空间时调整运行目标。两项新指标支撑了这一弹性决策：发电备用容量（GRC），用于量化可用于未来突发事件的剩余逆变器容量；以及受控性能退化（CPD），允许暂时、刻意地放宽性能要求，以维持系统的整体可操作性。由人选择的弹性调节参数 μ 决定了即时跟踪精度与长期运行就绪性之间的权衡。本文给出了所提出的 HOTL μ-mod 自适应控制器的稳定性分析。该方法还通过并网逆变器在连续执行器退化下的仿真得到了验证。结果表明，与传统的自适应控制和 FTC 方法相比，所提出的架构保留了控制备用，防止了执行器饱和，并实现了更优的电压调节。

## Abstract
This paper proposes a human-on-the-loop resilient control architecture for grid-supporting inverter-based resources (IBRs) operating under actuator degradation. Conventional fault-tolerant control and adaptive control strategies each face notable limitations in this setting: active FTC depends on fast, accurate fault detection and isolation, leaving it vulnerable to misdiagnosis of incipient or ambiguous degradation, while passive FTC tends toward overly conservative operation. Adaptive controllers face a related problem as they typically assume sufficient control authority, but when actuator degradation erodes that authority, the adaptive law may misinterpret tracking errors, leading to parameter drift, performance loss, or instability. To overcome these limitations, the proposed framework embeds human supervisory judgment directly into the control loop, detecting subtle off-nominal behavior, validating or overriding controller parameters, and adjusting operational objectives when conditions exceed the modeled fault space. Two new metrics underpin this resilient decision-making: Generation Reserve Capacity (GRC), which quantifies remaining inverter capacity available for future contingencies, and Controlled Performance Degradation (CPD), which allows temporary, deliberate performance relaxation to preserve overall system operability. A human-selected resilience tuning parameter, μ, governs the trade-off between immediate tracking accuracy and long-term operational readiness. A stability analysis of the proposed HOTL μ-mod adaptive controller is presented. The approach is also validated through simulations of a grid-connected inverter under sequential actuator degradation. Results show that the proposed architecture preserves control reserves, prevents actuator saturation, and achieves superior voltage regulation compared with conventional adaptive control and FTC methods.
