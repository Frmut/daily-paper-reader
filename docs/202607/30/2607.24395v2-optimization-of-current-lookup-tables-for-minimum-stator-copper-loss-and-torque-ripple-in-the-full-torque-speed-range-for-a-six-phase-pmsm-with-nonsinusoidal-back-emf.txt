Abstract —A recently proposed method was able to generate current references with minimum stator copper loss (SCL) and torque ripple over the full torque-speed range of multiphase non-salient permanent-magnet synchronous machines (PMSMs) with nonsinusoidal back-electromotive force (back-EMF). However, it relied on large lookup tables (LUTs) generated offline, requiring several hours for generation and large memory for storage. Since on-chip memory in industrial digital signal processors (DSPs) is limited, this hinders practical implementation. This paper addresses this problem by analyzing how the LUTs of that method can be simplified and optimized, focusing on the example of a symmetrical six-phase PMSM drive. Breakpoint selection (BS), LUT construction (LC) and interpolation approaches are evaluated to optimize the tradeoff between LUT simplification and performance. It is shown that the required memory and LUT generation time can be greatly reduced while keeping nearly the same feasible torque-speed area and performance. Simulation results confirm negligible performance degradation. 

Index Terms —Lookup table, maximum torque, minimum losses, multiphase, six-phase, speed range, voltage constraint. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Multiphase permanent-magnet synchronous machines (PMSMs) are well suited for applications requiring high reliability and torque density, as well as low per-phase power [1]. In particular, six-phase PMSMs offer a good tradeoff between these features and complexity [1], while symmetrical six-phase stator windings are advantageous over asymmetrical ones in terms of fault tolerance [2], dc-link capacitor size [1], etc. In addition, PMSMs with nonsinusoidal back-electromotive force (back-EMF), such as those based on fractional-slot concentrated windings (FSCWs), can further increase torque density [3]. Although FSCW PMSMs usually exhibit negligible saliency, they also offer high fault tolerance and reduced end windings [4]. To fully exploit the torque-speed capability of PMSMs driven by field-oriented control, suitable current references must be generated for the inner current controller according to the torque reference, actual speed and rotor position. Different current-reference generation techniques for multi-phase PMSMs with current and voltage constraints have been proposed [5]–[7]. However, they exhibit important drawbacks [2] such as unsuitability for symmetrical six-phase PMSMs [5], [7] or for any back-EMF harmonics [5], [7], inability to allow moderate torque ripple to further extend the torque-speed range [5]–[7], reduced efficiency and torque-speed capability due to fewer degrees of freedom [6], etc. In [2], a nonsinusoidal current-reference generation method overcoming these limitations was introduced for nonsalient PMSMs with any phase number, any sinusoidal or nonsinu-soidal back-EMF waveforms, and symmetrical or asymmetri-cal windings. It consists of an offline stage and an online stage. First, the optimum Fourier coefficients of the nonsinusoidal current references are found offline by solving a quadratic op-timization problem for multiple torque-speed operating points, and stored in lookup tables (LUTs) with uniformly spaced torque/speed breakpoints. This offline optimization minimizes stator copper loss (SCL) 1 and torque ripple per operating point, while mean-torque tracking is imposed through an equality constraint. Torque ripple, peak current, and peak voltage are limited, enabling operation over the full torque-speed range. In the online stage, the current references are generated from the LUTs by linear interpolation as a function of the torque reference and actual speed [2]. However, 4.4 MB was needed to store the ten required LUTs, assuming 16 bits per value [2]. This exceeds the available on-chip RAM space in most standard digital sig-nal processors (DSPs). Furthermore, solving the optimization problem to generate LUTs with so many entries took several hours despite using a powerful computer [2]. This paper addresses the simplification and optimization of the LUTs for the current-reference generation method from [2], focusing on a symmetrical star-connected six-phase PMSM drive with nonsinusoidal back-EMF. After review-ing the method from [2] (Section II), different possibilities for simplification of its original LUTs (OLUTs) are intro-duced (Section III), including various LUT-construction (LC), breakpoint-selection (BS) and interpolation approaches. Two main types of BS are considered: LUTs with uniformly spaced breakpoints of various resolutions, and LUTs with nonuniform spacing obtained by an alternative LUT reduction procedure. For the selected case-study parameters and metrics (Section IV), the reduced LUTs are evaluated (Section V) to optimize the tradeoff between LUT simplification and resulting performance. LUT simplification is assessed through generation time and memory, whereas the performance is evaluated through the reduction in the feasible torque-speed area under acceptable limits on peak line voltage, peak phase current, torque ripple and mean-torque deviation. Several options achieving a favorable compromise between simplicity and performance are identified and compared. The required memory and generation time are remarkably reduced, with a very small decrease in feasible torque-speed region. Sim-ulation results (Section VI) confirm negligible performance degradation, despite the remarkable LUT simplification. II. B. Offline Stage: OLUT Generation 

In the offline stage, the 2nh LUTs of IRe  

> h

and IIm  

> h

in Fig. 2 are generated by solving an optimization problem for each operating point ( T ∗, ω ), for subsequent storage in a DSP [2]. These LUTs are in principle rectangular, but only a tapered (T ∗, ω ) region is feasible and of interest [2]. To sweep the feasible operating points, T ∗ is increased from zero toward positive T ∗ with uniform steps ∆T , and for each T ∗, ω is increased from zero toward positive ω with uniform steps ∆ω,until no solution is found. The process is then repeated for 

T ∗ increasing from zero toward negative T ∗. Only ω ≥ 0 is considered, since the outputs for ω < 0 would be very similar to those of ω > 0 with opposite T ∗ sign. The optimization problem per ( T ∗, ω ) minimizes torque ripple with strict priority and, among the solutions with minimum torque ripple, the SCL. This is done while imposing the equality constraint that the mean permanent-magnet torque 

T equals that of the reference ( T = T ∗), as well as inequality constraints that limit the peak line voltage vpk , peak phase current ipk and torque ripple τ to the respective limits 3 [2]: 

T err = 0; vpk ≤ vmx pk ; ipk ≤ imx pk ; τ ≤ τ mx (3) where T err = T − T ∗ is mean T error. This is solved using MATLAB quadprog with negligible numerical tolerances. The ω breakpoints below ω↓ are removed, as the LUT values there do not vary with ω (the vpk constraint is inactive) [2]. III. D EFINITION OF LUT S IMPLIFICATION APPROACHES

> •

LC1 (downsampling): High-resolution OLUTs according to [2] using small steps ∆T and ∆ω are first generated. Reduced LUTs are then obtained by retaining only OLUT entries with steps λ∆T and λ∆ω, where λ is an integer.

LC2 (direct generation): High-resolution OLUTs are not generated. Instead, the offline LUT-generation procedure from [2] (Section II-B) is run at a reduced breakpoint set, using steps λ∆T and λ∆ω, to directly form the new LUTs. LC1 requires prior OLUT generation, whereas LC2 does not. To select the breakpoints, several BS approaches are defined next. BS1–BS3 and BS3B use (mostly) uniform spacing, whereas BS4 uses nonuniform spacing. BS1, BS2 and BS4 rely on LC1, whereas BS3 and BS3B rely on LC2. The reduced LUTs are evaluated for the BS, LC and interpolation approaches according to the introduced metrics. Fig. 6 shows the feasible T -ω area (for ϵ = 1% , 3% , and

The feasible T -ω area is normalized by that of the OLUTs, evaluated on the same dense grid and under the same 

ϵ. Fig. 6 was obtained by using just 16 bits per LUT entry (as for memory storage): 16 -bit signed fixed-point values allocated as 8 bits for the integer part and 8 bits for the fractional part. It was verified that the use of 64 -bit instead of 16 -bit LUT entries produces no noticeable differences. Fig. 6 shows that the LUT size can be greatly reduced with little performance loss. Using BS1 with λ = 17 [diamonds in Fig. 6(a)], each LUT is reduced from 389 ×790 ≈ 3·10 5 entries (stars) to 24 × 49 = 1176 , i.e., by a factor of 261 . Besides being simpler, linear interpolation is clearly the best option regarding feasible area. This was also verified for the other cases in Figs. 4 and 6. This is because the LUT surfaces, as shown in [2], are largely piecewise smooth, with extended flat regions and sharp slope changes near the activation of inequality constraints. Hence, linear interpolation preserves these features more faithfully, whereas higher-order methods tend to smooth or overshoot them. To sum up, Table I shows that the selected LUTs reduce the total memory of the ten LUTs from 6.1 MB to just 18 –

25 kB (i.e., by 340 –250 times), for any of the BS and LC approaches. Thus, the storage requirements of all the selected configurations can be expected to be compatible with industrial DSPs. If only the LUT entries of feasible points were stored, the size would be decreased from 4.4 MB of the OLUTs to values even lower than 18 –25 kB for the reduced LUTs, albeit at the cost of more complex indexing. On the other hand, the memory savings of the reduced LUTs is achieved while keeping nearly unaltered feasible T -ω area ( 97 .2–99 .8% ), with BS4 particularly excelling in this regard. Concerning offline LUT generation time, it is shortened from 574 min to only 

9–10 min , i.e., by about 64 times, 7 by using BS3 or BS3B (based on LC2). In all cases, linear interpolation is preferred. VI. S IMULATION RESULTS

This paper has addressed the simplification and optimization of the LUTs needed for a recent method of current-reference generation with minimum losses and torque ripple in the full torque-speed range of six-phase PMSMs with nonsinusoidal back-EMF. Different approaches to construct the LUTs (i.e., LC), to select the breakpoints (i.e., BS) and to perform inter-polation were evaluated for this purpose. Four BS approaches based on uniformly spaced torque/speed breakpoints (BS1– BS3 and BS3B) were considered, as well as an alternative iterative approach with nonuniform breakpoint spacing, BS4, based on removing, one at a time, the torque or speed breakpoint causing the smallest torque-speed-area loss. Several of them (BS1, BS2 and BS4) are based on downsampling existing high-resolution OLUTs (i.e., LC1), whereas BS3 and BS3B (relying on LC2) can be obtained without those OLUTs. Most importantly, the study showed that any of the selected approaches is able to drastically decrease the needed memory. Namely, the required memory was reduced from 6.1 MB 

(original) to 18 –25 kB by any of them. The LUT generation time was shortened from 574 min to 9–10 min by using BS3 or BS3B (i.e., LC2). Meanwhile, even with a stringent 1% ωm (kr/min)
