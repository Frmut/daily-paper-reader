Abstract 

We propose a robust exact sliding-mode controller for reference tracking with model-based disturbance rejection, where the disturbance is generated by a linear time-invariant model driven by a bounded input and may be bidirectionally coupled with the plant. The controller embeds the internal model principle and its error dynamics mirror those of the robust exact differentiator, ensuring finite-time convergence. We develop a proper implicit discretization for sampled-data implementation that preserves stability and accuracy. In the nominal case, which contains a disturbance generated by a p-th order integrator chain, the method achieves a control accuracy of T p+1 ¯D, with discretization time T and disturbance bound ¯D. This accuracy is shown to relate to the fundamental lower bound by a factor depending only on the order of the disturbance model. Simulations demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed methods. 

Key words: robust exact sliding-mode controller, super-twisting control, differentiator, chattering, sampled-data control 

1 Introduction 

Sliding-mode control (SMC) is an established robust control method against matched disturbances that ex-ploits discontinuities in the controller to stabilize the system state on a predefined sliding surface in finite time, i.e., drive a sliding variable to zero, see Slotine (1984); Utkin (1992). To mitigate chattering phenom-ena in case of sliding variables with relative degree one, higher-order sliding-mode (HOSM) control (HOSMC) was introduced that lifts the discontinuity to derivatives of the control input, see Levant (1993); Bartolini et al. (1998); Levant (2003). A side-effect of these HOSMC concepts is that the class of disturbances that can be re-jected by these controllers changes compared to the clas-sical first-order SMC. In particular, first-order SMC is robust against bounded disturbances, whereas second-order SMC like the super-twisting algorithm (STA) pro-

This Lipschitz-continuous distur-bance of the STA can be understood to be generated by an integrator driven by a bounded disturbance input, i.e., a first-order disturbance model. In parallel, a sliding-mode differentiator, called the robust exact differentia-tor (RED), was developed by Levant (1998, 2003) to es-timate derivatives of a signal in finite time. The RED incorporates a higher-order disturbance model consist-ing of a chain of integrators driven by a bounded distur-bance input. A topic that received a lot of attention in the past is the discrete-time implementation of sliding mode controllers. It is well-known that applying the straight-forward explicit Euler discretization to SMC leads to high-frequency oscillations, commonly known as dis-cretization chattering, in the steady state, deteriorating the control performance. Many publications were dedi-cated to analyzing discretized sliding-mode algorithms and developing discretization methods that avoid this form of chattering, see, e.g., Levant (2011); Livne and Levant (2014); Brogliato et al. (2018); Koch et al. (2019); Hanan et al. (2022); Andritsch et al. (2024); Seeber (2025). In this paper we generalize the application of the inter-nal model control principle in the design of sliding-mode controllers for sliding variables with relative degree one. We assume a disturbance generated by an arbitary lin-ear time-invariant system driven by a bounded distur-bance input and by the sliding variable itself. This dis-turbance may be unbounded and non-Lipschitz contin-uous, in comparison to existing approaches. A sliding-mode based control concept is proposed that robustly stabilizes the sliding variable in finite time. The result-ing closed-loop tracking error dynamics are equivalent to those of the arbitrary-order RED, leading to the deno-tion as robust exact sliding-mode controller (RESMC). The continuous-time controller design in this paper is a variation to the design presented by Andritsch et al. (2025). Furthermore, a proper implicit discretization of the RESMC is presented that avoids discretization chat-tering effects due to sampled-data control. After intro-ducing the control problem (Section 2), the contribu-tions of this paper are: 

• design of the RESMC for reference tracking (Sec-tion 3); 

• derivation of a system discretization (Section 4), that is sample-wise exact in the nominal case; 

• development of a proper implicit discretization of the RESMC that avoids discretization chattering (Section 5); 

• establishing a fundamental lower bound for the best possible worst-case control accuracy (Section 6). Simulations are demonstrated in Section 7. Finally, the paper is concluded in Section 8.

2 Problem Description 

In this paper we consider the reference tracking prob-lem of a scalar signal y following the linear disturbed dynamics ˙y = ay + b(u(t) + w(t)) , (1) with known and constant parameters a, b ∈ R, with b̸ =0, control input u(t) ∈ R and disturbance w(t) ∈ R. The control goal is that the output y(t) tracks the reference signal r(t) ∈ R that is differentiable at least once. The reference r(t) as well as its derivative ˙ r(t) are given. The dynamics of the tracking error σ(t) = y(t) − r(t) are ˙σ = ay − ˙r + b(u + w). (2) 

Remark 1 Often, the signal y(t) is the output with rel-ative degree one of a higher-order plant. Then, the states of the internal dynamics of this plant must stay bounded when y(t) tracks r(t). For details, see, e.g., Byrnes and Isidori (1989); Khalil (2002); Shtessel et al. (2014). In this paper, however, we focus on the simple first-order setup in (1) . The results can be transferred to controlling the relative degree one output of a higher-order system. 

We assume that the disturbance w(t) can be modeled by the known linear time-invariant system ˙φ = Sφ + f y + d∆( t),w = γ⊤φ, (3) with disturbance state φ(t) ∈ Rp, matrix S ∈ Rp×p,vectors f , d, γ ∈ Rp, and bounded Lebesgue-measurable unknown input ∆( t), i.e. |∆( t)| ≤ D ∀t ≥ 0. We assume that the output w(t) of the disturbance model (3) has relative degree p with respect to ∆( t). 

Remark 2 The well-known super-twisting controller by Levant (1993) is a controller for a special case of the presented setting with an integrator as disturbance model, i.e. (3) with p = 1 , S = 0 , f = 0 , d = 1 , and γ = 1 .Then, w(t) is a Lipschitz-continuous function with a bounded derivative. Also, the more general form of a sig-nal w(t) with a bounded p-th derivative, i.e. |w(p)| ≤ D,can be expressed via the disturbance model (3) with arbitrary p, S = 0, f = 0, d = ep, and γ = e1.

In the first part of this paper, we develop a continuous-time controller for the proposed control problem. After-wards, in the main part of this work, we assume that 2the control is applied to the plant via a zero-order hold element with constant hold time, i.e. 

u(t) = uk ∀t ∈ [kT, (k + 1) T ),yk = y(kT ), (4) with discretization time T > 0, discrete control sequence 

uk, k ∈ Z+0 , and samples yk of the system output y(t) at the same time-instances kT as the discrete-time control 

uk is applied. Only these samples yk, and not the entire continuous-time function y(t), are available to the con-troller. This control setting is known as sampled-data control in literature, see, e.g., Franklin et al. (1997).

5 Proper Implicit Discretization of the RESMC 

In this section, we derive an implicit discretization de-noted as proper implicit discretization of the continuous-time RESMC (17), (19), (20). The proper implicit dis-cretization consists of the following steps: (1) Derive a discrete-time plant description that is in implicit Euler form and provides a good approxi-mation of the sampled plant states (performed in Section 4). (2) Setup controller structure with dynamics in implicit Euler form and insert into the discrete-time system dynamics; (3) Define a state transformation such that the trans-formed state in the next step is implicitly known in the current step; (4) Design the controller terms such that the closed-loop dynamics of the transformed coordinates resemble the implicit Euler discretization of the continuous-time error dynamics. 

Remark 8 The discrete-time controller does not rely on a direct discretization of the controller itself. Instead, it is reconstructed in discrete time based on a discretization of the desired closed-loop error system. 

Remark 9 The third step described above is the funda-mental difference to other implicit discretizations in lit-erature, see, e.g., Brogliato et al. (2020); Huber et al. (2016). There, the authors neglect unknown information to obtain an approximation for the state in the next sam-pling instance. Such an approximation via neglecting un-knowns is not necessary in the presented approach. 

Remark 10 All equations that must be implemented to obtain the proposed proper implicit RESMC are embedded in green boxes.

For p = 1 this was also shown in (Seeber and Andritsch, 2025, Propo-sition 2.1). However, for p > 1, according to Proposi-tion 17, there might exist a discrete-time controller that achieves a smaller accuracy band than T p+1 D.

8 Conclusion 

In this paper we presented a robust exact sliding-mode controller together with its proper implicit discretiza-tion that is able to reject disturbances generated by an LTI model with bounded input. The discretization is ob-tained by redesigning the controller in discrete time so that the implicit-Euler closed-loop error dynamics of the continuous-time design are reproduced in transformed coordinates. In the nominal case the accuracy of the dis-cretization is related to the best possible worst-case ac-curacy by a factor depending only on the disturbance model order, independent of the sampling time. Simu-lations confirm the theory. Contrary to common belief, 12 very small discretization times are not strictly neces-sary when sliding-mode control exploits advanced con-trol concepts and implementation schemes.
