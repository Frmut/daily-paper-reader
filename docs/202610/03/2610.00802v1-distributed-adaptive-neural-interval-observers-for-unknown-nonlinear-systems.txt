# Distributed Adaptive Neural Interval Observers for Unknown Nonlinear Systems 

Tien Dat Vu, My Nguyen Bach, Phuoc Vinh Nguyen and Minh Doan 

Abstract —This paper develops a distributed adaptive neural interval observer for unknown nonlinear systems with locally incomplete measurements. Each sensor node constructs lower and upper state estimates using its local output and neighboring observer information, while unknown nonlinear dynamics are ap-proximated by adaptive neural models. A distributed adaptation mechanism guarantees bounded estimation and weight errors, whereas a cooperative realization preserves the componentwise interval property. To enhance neural-weight convergence without requiring persistent excitation, a finite experience-replay integral concurrent-learning mechanism is incorporated into the adap-tation law. For non-Metzler error dynamics, a Sylvester-based coordinate transformation is introduced to recover a Hurwitz– Metzler distributed realization. The theoretical developments are validated through a nonlinear distributed estimation example. 

Index Terms —Distributed observer, interval observer, neural networks, nonlinear systems, adaptive estimation, positive sys-tems. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Reliable state estimation becomes difficult when a nonlinear plant is observed through spatially distributed sensors, each having access only to partial output information. A con-ventional distributed observer may recover the state through local innovations and inter-node information exchange, but it generally provides a point estimate rather than a guaran-teed state enclosure. Conversely, interval observers construct 

x(t) ≤ x(t) ≤ ¯x(t) under bounded uncertainty, with the enclosure typically obtained from cooperative error dynamics [1]–[3]. The problem considered here is therefore to preserve such an interval simultaneously at every sensor node when the nonlinear dynamics are unknown and no individual sensor is required to be detectable. Distributed state estimation has been extensively studied for linear systems with partial sensing and network communica-tion [4], [5]. More recently, distributed interval observers have been developed for uncertain LTI and descriptor systems under joint or collective sensing conditions [6], [7]. In parallel, neural networks have been incorporated into interval observers to approximate unknown nonlinear functions [8]. These develop-ments, however, leave a gap between distributed interval esti-mation and adaptive learning of unknown nonlinear dynamics: existing distributed interval constructions remain essentially model-based, whereas neural interval observers are primarily centralized and their adaptation is not derived from the dis-tributed Lyapunov structure. Moreover, parameter convergence in conventional adaptive schemes typically requires persistent excitation, which can be restrictive in practical online esti-mation. Concurrent-learning methods alleviate this require-ment by repeatedly exploiting sufficiently informative recorded data, while integral concurrent learning further replaces the persistent-excitation condition by an online-verifiable finite-data richness condition [9], [10]. This paper addresses this gap by constructing a distributed adaptive neural interval observer under collective detectability. The main contributions are summarized as follows. First ,

A. Graph-Theoretic Preliminaries 

The communication among the N sensing nodes is described by a weighted directed graph G = ( V, E, A), where V := 

{1, . . . , N } is the node set, E ⊆ V × V is the directed edge set, and A = [ aij ] ∈ RN ×N is the weighted adjacency matrix. An edge (j, i ) ∈ E means that node i receives information from node j, in which case aij > 0; otherwise aij = 0 ,and aii = 0 . The in-neighbor set of node i is Ni := {j ∈V : ( j, i ) ∈ E} = {j ∈ V : aij > 0}, and its weighted in-degree is di := PNj=1 aij . The corresponding degree matrix is 

D := diag {d1, . . . , d N }, and the graph Laplacian is defined as L := D − A . Hence, Lii = di and Lij = −aij for i̸ = j,so that L1N = 0 . Throughout the paper, information available to node i consists of its local measurement together with the signals received from nodes in Ni.

C. Distributed Sensing and Communication 

Node i measures only yi = Cix; hence, the individual pair (A, C i) is not required to be detectable. Define the aggregate output matrix CV := col {C1, . . . , C N } and yV := col {y1, . . . , y N } = CV x. The aggregate quantity yV is intro-duced only for analysis and is not available at any individual node. 

Definition 1 (Collective detectability) : The sensing network is collectively detectable if (A, C V ) is detectable, i.e., for every 

λ ∈ σ(A) with Re( λ) ≥ 0, rank col {λI n − A, C V } = n.Thus, an unstable mode may be unobservable from some 

Ci, but it must be detectable from the sensing network as a whole. 

Assumption 4: The graph G is fixed and strongly connected, with aij > 0 for every (j, i ) ∈ E .

Assumption 5: The pair (A, C V ) is collectively detectable according to Definition 1. At node i, the admissible online information is 

{yi, u, d, ¯d, x i, ¯xi, W i, ¯Wi, x j , ¯xj : j ∈ Ni}. Thus, no node requires the full output yV or the true state x.

Assumption 6: Neighboring information is transmitted con-tinuously without delay or packet loss. The communication topology is fixed during the interval of operation. Collective detectability and interval preservation are distinct but complementary requirements in the distributed interval observer design. The former ensures that the sensing network as a whole contains sufficient information to detect the relevant plant modes through the pair (A, C V ), whereas the latter guarantees that the upper and lower estimates preserve the required componentwise ordering over the entire network. In particular, the cooperative error matrix in (7) must be Metzler, i.e., all of its off-diagonal entries are nonnegative [1]–[3], in order for the distributed interval observer to preserve the interval enclosure.

The neural interval estimates in Figs. 2–5 show that the initial enclosures contract rapidly within approximately the first 30 − 50 s and subsequently remain very tight for all four states. In contrast, the nominal interval observer with-out neural compensation in Figs. 6–9 converges only to a considerably wider nonzero tube because the nominal model does not compensate for the unknown nonlinear dynamics. The adaptive-weight trajectories in Figs. 10–13 become nearly stationary after approximately 100 −200 s , while the network-wide comparison in Fig. 14 shows that the neural-compensated interval widths are reduced by more than one order of mag-nitude relative to the nominal case after the initial transient. These tighter distributed intervals are directly relevant to fault-detection and isolation applications, since they provide less conservative residual thresholds for detecting sensor, actuator, or communication faults in networked systems. VI. C ONCLUSION 

This paper developed a distributed adaptive neural interval observer for unknown nonlinear systems. The proposed de-sign guarantees bounded transformed and physical-state inter-val estimation errors together with bounded adaptive neural weights, while the stored-data learning mechanism improves parameter convergence without requiring persistent excitation of the online regressors. The simulation results verify that the neural-compensated intervals contract to significantly tighter bounds than the nominal interval observer and that the adaptive weights approach steady values under finite excitation. These results support the effectiveness of the proposed framework for distributed state estimation and fault-detection applications in networked nonlinear systems. ACKNOWLEDGMENT
