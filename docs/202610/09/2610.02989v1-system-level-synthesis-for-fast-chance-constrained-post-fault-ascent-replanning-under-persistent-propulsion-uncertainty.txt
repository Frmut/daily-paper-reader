A nonfatal propulsion fault can invalidate the nominal ascent trajectory while leaving suf-ficient vehicle capability to reach the original orbit or a degraded target. Deterministic re-planning can restore nominal feasibility, but residual uncertainty in the identified propulsion capability may still lead to path-constraint violations and terminal injection dispersion. This paper develops a fast chance-constrained stochastic ascent trajectory replanner that explicitly accounts for persistent propulsion uncertainty. The uncertainty model combines persistent random uncertainties shared across the prediction horizon with interval-wise independent dis-turbances. For a fixed nominal trajectory, system level synthesis is used to parameterize the closed-loop responses. A Cholesky-based transformation of the system responses converts the resulting cross-time correlated feedback design problem into column-wise affine recursions that share a common Riccati recursion. A task-oriented terminal weight is further derived from the minimum-energy post-injection orbital correction in phase-free modified equinoctial elements, thereby relating terminal covariance shaping directly to correction demand. The stochastic replanning problem is solved by alternating deterministic trajectory replanning and feedback controller design, with covariance-based chance-constraint backoffs coupling the two updates. Nonlinear Monte Carlo simulations demonstrate reduced equivalent correction de-mand, effective chance-constraint satisfaction, and fast online replanning over a range of un-certainty levels and fault conditions.

## I. Introduction 

# MODERN launch vehicles often employ multi-engine propulsion systems. As the number of engines increases, the possibility of an engine fault must be explicitly considered in ascent guidance design, motivating guidance meth-ods that can complete the mission after a nonfatal propulsion failure. When the remaining capability is sufficient, a reduction in thrust or specific impulse may still permit injection into the original orbit or a degraded rescue orbit, but the ascent trajectory must be recomputed to match the changed propulsion capability. Engine-out guidance has therefore long been part of ascent guidance and mission retargeting [1, 2]. Sequential convex programming has recently received considerable attention for computationally efficient trajectory optimization [3, 4], and has consequently been adopted for online ascent trajectory replanning [5–8]. Existing post-fault replanning methods, however, are predominantly deterministic: once a fault has been diagnosed, the identified propulsion capability is generally treated as exactly known during trajectory replanning. In practice, the reconstructed post-fault dynamics remain subject to both epistemic and aleatory uncertainties. Epistemic uncertainty arises from incomplete knowledge of physical quantities and model parameters, including residual errors in the iden-tified fault magnitude, whereas aleatory uncertainty represents intrinsic variability in the physical system and environ-ment [9, 10]. This issue is particularly relevant to propulsion faults because existing studies indicate that the residual uncertainty in identified thrust can remain appreciably larger than that associated with reconstructed kinematic states [11–14]. Consequently, nonnegligible uncertainty may remain in the identified propulsion capability even after the 2fault has been successfully diagnosed. In particular, a residual error in the identified mean thrust can persist throughout the remaining burn, producing a sustained effect whose influence on the trajectory can accumulate over time. Neglect-ing such residual uncertainty may therefore lead to path-constraint violations or excessive terminal injection dispersion when the vehicle tracks a trajectory obtained from deterministic replanning. Accounting for residual uncertainty during replanning requires predicting how it propagates into trajectory disper-sion. Nonlinear uncertainty propagation can be performed using Monte Carlo simulation [15, 16], polynomial chaos expansions [17–19], the unscented transform [20, 21], or higher-order state transition tensors [22, 23]. These methods can retain nonlinear effects beyond a first-order local covariance approximation, but generally require greater computa-tional cost. For time-critical onboard replanning, linear covariance propagation provides a computationally attractive alternative by propagating covariance through locally linearized closed-loop dynamics and has been widely used in guidance, navigation, and mission analysis [24–26]. A practical strategy for real-time stochastic trajectory planning is therefore to use linear covariance propagation within the onboard optimization, while reserving nonlinear Monte Carlo simulation for offline validation and performance assessment. Representative computational strategies based on linear covariance models include the following. One strategy uses a horizon-wide causal affine feedback parameterization, in which the block-lower-triangular feedback map over the pre-diction horizon is optimized explicitly [27–30]. A second strategy takes nodal covariance matrices as the primary stochastic variables and convexifies the covariance dynamics by relaxing the nonlinear propagation equality into linear matrix inequalities [31, 32]. The third separates nominal trajectory optimization from feedback design and alternates between the two updates, allowing the feedback step to retain a Riccati recursion [33–36]. This separation is attrac-tive for real-time replanning because it avoids introducing the complete feedback design into the nominal trajectory optimization. To extend this computationally efficient alternating framework to stochastic post-fault ascent replanning, two limi-tations must still be addressed. First, existing fast Riccati-based formulations typically model uncertainty as temporally independent process noise and therefore do not capture the cross-time dependence induced by a persistent bias [33–36]. A covariance steering method proposed by Knaup and Tsiotras explicitly accounts for unknown constant parameters but requires sequential convex programming [37]. Another approach is to include the persistent uncertain quantity in an augmented state [38, 39]. This restores a Markov representation but requires sufficiently accurate online es-timation of the augmented quantity for feedback implementation, which is difficult to obtain for residual post-fault thrust uncertainty. Thus, retaining persistent uncertainty while preserving Riccati-structured computation and avoid-ing dependence on high-accuracy online parameter estimation remains challenging. Second, simply reducing terminal covariance does not determine which combinations of terminal errors require the greatest post-injection correction effort. Minimum-maneuver and control-distance metrics have been used in orbital maneuver analysis to quantify the physical consequence of orbital state differences [40–43]. Such metrics are well suited for performance evaluation, but they are generally formulated as optimization problems and do not directly provide the quadratic terminal weight required for covariance-based feedback design. A remaining challenge is therefore to translate the required maneuver effort into a terminal covariance penalty that can directly guide feedback synthesis. The contributions of this work are as follows. First, we formulate the stochastic feedback problem using system level synthesis (SLS) [44–47], which parameterizes the closed-loop responses from exogenous disturbances to states and inputs. For systems subject to both persistent uncertainty and interval-wise independent disturbances, a Cholesky-based transformation of the system responses decomposes the resulting cross-time correlated feedback design problem into column-wise affine recursions that share a common Riccati recursion. Second, we derive a task-oriented terminal weight from the orbital correction required after injection. A phase-free modified equinoctial element mapping relates terminal state errors to candidate correction impulses and yields an analytic quadratic weight for feedback design. Finally, the two developments are integrated into an online stochastic ascent replanner that alternates deterministic trajectory replanning and feedback controller design, with each deterministic update implemented as one real-time iteration and the two steps coupled through covariance-based chance-constraint backoffs. The complete replanner is demonstrated through nonlinear Monte Carlo simulations. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section II defines the stochastic ascent trajectory-replanning problem and its local chance-constraint approximation. Section III develops the fast system level synthesis formulation for persistent uncertainty. Section IV derives the Task-Oriented terminal weight. Section V describes the alternating online replanning procedure. Section VI presents the nonlinear numerical results, followed by conclusions. 3II. Stochastic Ascent Trajectory Replanning Problem

## V. Real-Time Stochastic Replanning 

A. Alternating replanning and mission logic 

The overall replanning workflow is shown in Fig. 3. At each stochastic outer iteration, the nominal problem P1

is alternated with the transformed feedback problem P2c , from which the regularized physical response is recovered according to Theorem 1. To meet the real-time requirement, the nominal update uses a real-time iteration (RTI), defined here as one warm-started convex SCP subproblem rather than a fully converged deterministic trajectory solve at every outer iteration. Given the current chance-constraint backoffs, the nominal RTI updates P1. For an accepted nominal trajectory, the local dynamics are relinearized, the process weights in Eq. (24) are refreshed, and P2c is solved to update the closed-loop responses. The resulting covariance from Eq. (38) is then used to recompute the chance-constraint backoffs for the next nominal update. The outer iteration terminates when both the scaled nominal-trajectory change and the normalized change in the backoff vector satisfy their prescribed tolerances, or when the maximum number of outer iterations, 𝑁 max ,is reached. Related local convergence and stationary-point analyses of alternating backoff updates are available for Riccati-ZORO [34]. The present algorithm is evaluated without assuming global convergence of the complete nonlinear al-ternating iteration. Failure of the stochastic outer iteration to converge does not invalidate the last accepted nominal trajectory. That trajectory may remain dynamically feasible and satisfy the deterministic constraints, while the newly evaluated stochastic backoffs are not satisfied and the corresponding chance-constraint requirements therefore remain unmet. For engineering robustness, a deterministic replanning branch without stochastic backoffs can also be solved in parallel and retained as a fallback if the stochastic outer iteration does not converge. A three-level homotopy on the 𝑞𝛼 bound is used in cases where the post-fault warm start lies far from the final aerodynamic-load limit. The homotopy proceeds from a relaxed 𝑞𝛼 bound to the final bound, with each tighter level warm-started from the last accepted trajectory. If a nominal RTI update fails or is rejected, the most recently accepted trajectory and backoffs are retained, the homotopy level is relaxed by one step subject to its lowest level, and the SCP penalty weights are adjusted for the next outer iteration. For the elliptical-orbit mission considered here, the original target and degraded target nominal problems corre-spond to Problems 1 and 3 of Ref. [7], respectively. The two branches can be computed speculatively in parallel from 15 the same flight state and identified post-fault propulsion model. Mission selection nevertheless remains priority-gated: the original target is retained whenever it satisfies the prescribed acceptance conditions within its outer-iteration budget, and the degraded-target solution is authorized only otherwise. The method is intended for the residual uncertainty remaining after a useful propulsion diagnosis. If subsequent measurements materially change the estimated propulsion capability or its uncertainty, the replanning procedure can be repeated from the updated flight state.

## VII. Conclusions 

This paper developed a fast chance-constrained stochastic ascent trajectory replanner for nonfatal propulsion faults with residual uncertainty after diagnosis. The uncertainty model distinguishes a persistent component shared across the prediction horizon from interval-wise independent disturbances. For a fixed nominal trajectory, system level syn-thesis is used to parameterize the closed-loop responses. A Cholesky-based transformation of the system responses decomposes the resulting cross-time correlated feedback-design problem into column-wise affine recursions that share a common Riccati recursion, thereby retaining the persistent uncertainty structure while preserving fast Riccati-based computation. In addition, a task-oriented terminal weight is derived from the minimum-energy post-injection orbital correction in phase-free modified equinoctial elements, relating terminal covariance shaping directly to subsequent correction demand. These developments are integrated into an online stochastic ascent replanner that alternates deter-ministic trajectory replanning and feedback controller design, with the two updates coupled through covariance-based chance-constraint backoffs. The numerical results first verify the effectiveness of the proposed post-injection correction metric. The correction demand becomes nearly insensitive to further increases in the number of candidate correction locations beyond the adopted setting, while nonlinear replay confirms that the computed correction impulses effectively remove the corre-sponding orbital errors. For the representative fault case, introducing the task-oriented terminal weight reduces the mean post-injection correction demand by 53 .6% relative to the diagonal terminal weight. Explicitly retaining persis-tent cross-time correlation through the full-history SLS feedback provides a further average reduction of 24 .1% over the tested propulsion-uncertainty range. Across the fault cases covering different fault times and fault severities, the longest reported replanning time is 0.623 s , and the average computation time per outer iteration is 58 .92 ms . These results demonstrate that persistent uncertainty and task-oriented terminal covariance shaping can be incorporated into post-fault ascent replanning while retaining computational efficiency for online implementation.
