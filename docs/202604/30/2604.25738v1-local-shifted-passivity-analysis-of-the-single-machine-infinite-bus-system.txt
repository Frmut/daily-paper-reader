# Local Shifted Passivity Analysis of the Single-Machine Infinite-Bus System 

Xinyuan Jiang 

> Independent Researcher, State College, PA 16803, USA

Abstract 

This letter presents a shifted passivity analysis of the single-machine infinite-bus system in the stationary ( αβ ) reference frame. We study the attractivity of a periodic synchronous steady state with constant rotor frequency and formulate shifted passivity with respect to this motion. A port-Hamiltonian representation of the machine dynamics is used to construct a local shifted passivity condition from the error Hamiltonian and a correction term adapted to the synchronous steady state. For the infinite-bus interconnection, the resulting dissipation inequality leads to a sufficient stability condition expressed in terms of field excitation magnitude, damping, inertia, and steady-state current. This condition implies local asymptotic stability of the synchronous steady state and yields a sublevel-set estimate of its region of attraction under an additional small-inertia condition. A distinctive feature of the analysis is that it preserves the periodic structure of the rotor angle and provides a compact passivity-based stability certificate for the stationary-frame model. 

Keywords: Synchronous generators; Power system stability; Port-Hamiltonian systems; Shifted passivity; Periodic solutions 

1. Introduction 

Characterizing when a power system returns to synchronous operation after a disturbance is a basic problem in stability theory. For synchronous generators, Lyapunov and energy-based methods have long been the main analytical tools because they directly connect the physical model to rigorous stability certificates. In the classical lossless setting, the electrical power balance can be embedded into an energy function, and local stability is then tied to the geometry of this function around a synchronous operating point [1, 2]. This perspective underlies the standard direct methods and also more recent passivity- and Bregman-based formulations [3, 4, 5, 6]. A limitation of the usual energy-function approach is that the mechanical power input is often handled through angle-linear terms. To account for the mechanical power source that destroys the monotonic decay of candidate Lyapunov functions, one often introduces terms that are linear in the rotor angles [3, 4]. These terms are natural in Euclidean coordinates, but the angle variables themselves are periodic. As a result, the energy function is discontinuous in the physical angle space, and one must recover stability through local convexity arguments or additional bounds on the angular motion [7, 8, 1]. In this sense, the analytical convenience of the construction can obscure the underlying periodic geometry of the model. A related difficulty is that many local stability certificates encode the operating point through angle-difference or power-transfer constraints [9, 4, 6]. While such conditions are insightful from a

power-flow perspective, they are not always expressed directly in terms of the power supply and dissipation structure of the machine, so they cannot be enforced for each machine. This letter revisits the classical single-machine infinite-bus (SMIB) system from a different angle. We work in the stationary αβ frame and consider a synchronous steady state not as an equilibrium in rotating coordinates, but as a periodic motion with constant rotor frequency. For this reference motion, the natural system theoretic property to prove is a shifted dissipation inequality with respect to a periodic trajectory rather than with respect to an equilibrium point. The key observation is that shifted dissipation can be implemented in a way that preserves the periodicity of the rotor angle. Using the complex phasor to embed the rotor angle, we express the machine dynamics in a port-Hamiltonian form and start from the physical stored energy. By introducing a suitable correction term, we obtain a shifted storage function whose derivative along trajectories can be estimated by a quadratic dissipation inequality. For the infinite-bus interconnec-tion, the supply term vanishes, and the remaining condition can be written explicitly in terms of field excitation magnitude, damping, inertia, and steady-state current. This yields a local shifted passivity condition for the stationary-frame model of a constantly excited synchronous generator. From this condition, we obtain local asymptotic stability of the synchronous steady state and a sublevel-set estimate of its region of attraction. The resulting argument is based on the physical dissipation structure of the model and avoids introducing angle-linear energy terms that break the natural periodicity of the state. The main contribution is proving the local shifted passivity condition relative to a periodic synchronous motion of the stationary-frame SMIB model. Unlike usual energy functions with angle-linear terms, the proposed storage is defined on the embedded angle manifold and remains periodic in the rotor angle. The resulting certificate does not explicitly impose angle-difference or coupling-strength constraints of the type appearing in some energy-function analyses [9, 4]. In view of port-Hamiltonian modeling of large-scale power networks, this dissipativity-based form may serve as a building block for future interconnection-based analyses of multimachine systems with suitable load models [10, 11, 12, 13].

6. Conclusion 

This letter examined the local shifted passivity of a single-machine infinite-bus system with respect to a synchronous periodic orbit in the stationary frame. The analysis addresses a feature that is absent from the usual equilibrium-shifted setting: the reference motion exchanges nonzero steady-state power with the bus, and phase displacement relative to this motion produces residual electromagnetic coupling terms. The main result is a local shifted passivity condition for this periodic setting. Starting from a port-Hamiltonian representation, a correction term is added to the shifted Hamiltonian to cancel a drift term induced by the rotating electromagnetic coupling. The resulting storage function remains periodic in the rotor angle and yields a local dissipation inequality. For the infinite-bus intercon-nection, this inequality implies local asymptotic stability of the synchronous motion and gives a sublevel-set estimate of its region of attraction. Beyond the specific inequalities obtained here, the analysis suggests that shifted passivity can provide a complementary viewpoint to classical energy-function arguments for synchronous-machine models with periodic state variables. In particular, it avoids introducing angle-dependent terms that are convenient analytically but do not respect the natural geometry of the state space. From this viewpoint, the present result should be understood less as a global characterization of SMIB stability and more as a local geometric certificate that is connected to the dissipative structure of the machine. 9Appendix A.
